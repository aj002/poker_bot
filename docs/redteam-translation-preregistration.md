# Pre-registration: off-tree price translation in the final bot

Written and committed before any run of the specs below. Everything that motivated the
candidate came from red-team discovery runs (`strategy/redteam`): exploiter bots against
explicit tables of the target, the blueprint-only bundle and nlhe-v2. No population member
of any partition sat at those tables. The holdout partition is not used.

## Question

Does reading an off-tree bet as the lower neighbouring price bucket, with pseudo-harmonic
probability, make the final bot score more against bet-size translation exploiters,
without losing meaningfully elsewhere?

## The leak

The blueprint's price feature is `to_call / (pot + to_call)` in buckets with edges 3/20,
1/4, 1/3 and 2/5 (`strategy/blueprint/abstraction.py`). A value on an edge stays in the
lower bucket. The trained half-pot and pot bets price at exactly 1/4 and 1/3, so they sit on
the top edge of their buckets. Any bet between two trained sizes is read as the larger one:
a 0.22-pot bet as a half-pot bet, a 0.51-pot bet as a pot bet. The target then defends as if
it were getting the worse price. `SizingExploitBot` (`strategy/redteam/bots.py`) plays the
target's own blueprint but bets the smallest amount that gives every opponent left to act
the same key. That shrinks postflop bets to 55–70% of their chips.

Discovery (96 blocks × 8 seat rotations, each a table of the exploiter and seven copies of
the opponent; points taken per tournament from a table of the opponent, 95% CI):

| exploiter | vs target | vs blueprint-only | vs nlhe-v2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sizing-all | +0.53 [−0.67, +1.74] | +0.26 [−0.93, +1.45] | +2.30 [+1.11, +3.49] |
| sizing-bluff | +0.50 [−0.56, +1.57] | +0.94 [−0.07, +1.94] | +3.23 [+1.95, +4.52] |
| static (no shrink, same layer) | −0.23 [−1.22, +0.76] | +0.15 [−0.82, +1.12] | +1.69 [+0.43, +2.95] |

The leak is small and not significant on its own. This test asks whether closing it is
worth anything.

## What is fixed before the runs

- **Arms.** A is the final bot with the HandHQ exploit pack, built from this commit:
  `python -m strategy.final.package --out artifacts/redteam/target-base --exploit-pack
  strategy/tables/data/handhq/exploit-practice_nlhe.npz`, bundle sha256
  `0c1a911908ceb3f5684203f8f1b89de771a5c0ce5d9f83d5470f94e9e14e1b31`. B is the same command
  with `--override translate=postflop --out artifacts/redteam/target-translate`, bundle sha256
  `fbde598b3490f6e10d85ecacc4b7d2b8c5c85d0ce190ef208da8ba3b1a99f847`. The two bundles differ
  in one file, `bot.py`, which passes `overrides={'translate': 'postflop'}` to `FinalBot`.
  A plays exactly like the 841a9ec bundle (sha256 `c3e0fa54…`), because the flag defaults
  to off. The bundles differ from 841a9ec's only in `strategy/blueprint/runtime.py` and
  `strategy/exploit/runtime.py`, where the flag lives.
- **Candidate.** `translate=postflop` changes only postflop decisions that face a bet. Let
  `x = to_call / (pot − to_call)` be the bet's price-equivalent fraction of the pot, and let
  `A < x ≤ B` be the fractions at the edges of its price bucket (0.214, 0.5, 1 and 2, for
  price buckets 2–4). The bot then reads the bet as the bucket below with probability
  `(B − x)(1 + A) / ((B − A)(1 + x))`. That probability is 0 at a trained size
  (`x = B`) and close to 1 just above the lower edge. The draw comes from a stream keyed by
  the policy seed and the decision id (`lower_price_probability`,
  `BlueprintPolicy.play_features`). Bets in the smallest bucket or above two pots are read
  as before, and so is every preflop decision.
- **Exploiters for H1.** `artifacts/redteam/handhq-v1/bots/sizing-all.py` (sha256
  `08e0d4620b1c0840…`) and `sizing-bluff.py` (`f314727277afce67…`), generated by
  `python -m strategy.redteam.run measure`, on the target's blueprint pack.
- **Seeds.** Each spec's `base_seed` is `<experiment_id>-v1`. No discovery run used these
  seeds.

## Primary analysis

Four co-primary hypotheses on B − A, in mean terminal points per candidate seat. Every spec
uses 8-player NLHE tables with 2,000 chips, a 200-hand cap and every seat rotation per block,
in-process, 7 jobs:

| id | spec | opponents | hypothesis | blocks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | `redteam-translate-sizing-validation` | 7 × sizing-all; 7 × sizing-bluff | superiority: H0 mean ≤ 0 | 2 × 400 |
| H2 | `redteam-translate-population-validation` | 16 sampled `pop-v1+handhq-v1` validation lineups | non-inferiority: H0 mean ≤ −0.5 | 16 × 50 |
| H3 | `redteam-translate-robust-equilibrium` | the R1 lineups (blueprint bundles, capped-leaf blueprints, endgame bots) | non-inferiority: H0 mean ≤ −0.5 | 3 × 100 |
| H4 | `redteam-translate-robust-deceptive` | the R2 lineups (bait, image, flip, mix, arena switcher) | non-inferiority: H0 mean ≤ −0.5 | 6 × 50 |

H3 uses the R1 lineups of `handhq-exploit-robust-equilibrium`. The blueprint bundle there is
`artifacts/redteam/handhq-v1/arms/blueprint-only`: the target's blueprint pack, packaged by
`strategy.blueprint.package`.

Each run is `python -m strategy.exploit.evaluate run <spec> --jobs 7`. The test is the
one-sided paired t-test on block differences in `python -m strategy.exploit.evaluate
confirm --sup H1=… --ni H2=… --ni H3=… --ni H4=… --margin 0.5`, with Holm correction at
family-wise α = 0.05. Discovery runs put the per-block SD near 6 points, so H1 has 80%
power at about +0.65 points.

**Deployed gates** (on an otherwise idle machine, B's bundle):

- `python -m strategy.policy.gate --bundle artifacts/redteam/target-translate --decisions
  50000`: every check ok.
- `python -m strategy.final.package --verify --out artifacts/redteam/target-translate`: ok.

**Decision.** Recommend shipping `translate=postflop` only if H1 is a win, H2, H3 and H4 all
pass, and both gates pass. Otherwise the flag stays available and off. Either way, this
change does not alter the default bundle.
